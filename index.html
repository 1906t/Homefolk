The concept

A small home. A real community.

A tiny-home is a compact, low-carbon house. Cluster around eight of them with shared kitchen, laundry and garden, and you get a tiny village — close-knit, far lighter on the planet than a standard home.

Because the pods are light-touch and need no foundations, they can sit on paved, underused land and be fully removed later. It's been done in towns before — never in a dense city like London.

~8 homes a pilot village of tiny-homes plus communal facilities, grown from an existing neighbourhood so residents have a support network from day one.

Built to a real standard

A pilot, taken seriously.

This is an early-stage proposal — and it's being designed against real safety, accessibility and London planning standards from the start.

Ownership

Owned by the community, forever.

homefolk layers three proven models so homes stay genuinely affordable for generations — an approach championed by former UN housing rapporteur Prof. Raquel Rolnik.

Community Land Trust

CLT

Separates the land from the building — residents pay only for the home, locking in affordability.

Cooperative

COOP

Residents are shareholders with a secure right to live there for as long as they hold their share.

Community Interest Company

CIC

A trusted company form that puts community benefit, not profit, first.

St Clements · Tower HamletsLondon's first-ever CLT site: 252 new homes, 35% genuinely affordable, by and for local people.
LILAC · LeedsA low-impact co-housing community of 20 straw-and-timber eco-homes around a shared common house.
